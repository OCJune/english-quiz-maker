A Morning at the Riverside Library by Daniel Park. As the coordinator of our reading circle Pages Together, I arranged a short visit to the riverside library for our members. A community library is a quiet place where students, retired teachers, and curious neighbours can read and talk without hurry. Every member of the circle agreed that the library would be a suitable place to compare our reading notes. Curious about the old collection, we set out early on Saturday. March 14, Saturday Our circle gathered at the Riverside Reading Room. Helen, the librarian responsible for the rare book shelf, greeted us with a warm smile and offered a tour of the upper floor. It was surprising to see maps and letters from the last century resting under glass. Our tasks for the morning included sorting donated novels and labelling the shelves for younger readers. While sorting the donations, we checked whether any pages were torn or missing. Then, we prepared the display by wiping the wooden frames and arranging them into several neat rows. For fragile volumes with loose bindings, we used cotton tape instead of the plastic covers that we normally apply. Spending the whole morning helping out with the collection was a satisfying experience for me. It was a rewarding morning, and I was impressed with the patience the librarians showed toward every visitor.
